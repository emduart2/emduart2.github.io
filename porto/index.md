## Possible topics

**Algebraic statistics:** graphical models, causality, maximum likelihood estimation, models with hidden variables.<br>
**Applied algebraic geometry:** geometric modeling, computer algebra, toric varieties, virtual resolutions.

## How to get here

**PhD positions.** The [Fundação para a Ciência e a Tecnologia (FCT)](https://www.fct.pt) opens a call for doctoral scholarships every year. Contact me well before the deadline so we can shape a project together.

**Postdoctoral fellowships.** [Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellowships](https://marie-sklodowska-curie-actions.ec.europa.eu/actions/postdoctoral-fellowships) let you choose your host institution, and Porto can be it. FCT also funds researcher contracts through its Scientific Employment Stimulus calls.

**Students.** Students at European universities can come for a semester through [Erasmus+](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu). At Porto, I supervise BSc and MSc theses on the topics above.

**Visits.** Short visits and seminar talks can usually be arranged. Write to me with your dates and what you would like to work on.

## Why Porto

**The mathematics.** The [Centro de Matemática da Universidade do Porto (CMUP)](https://www.cmup.pt) hosts research in algebra, geometry, combinatorics, analysis, probability and statistics, with a lively seminar programme and international visitors.

**The city.** Porto sits on the Douro river and the Atlantic coast. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the food is excellent, and the climate is mild all year. Life here is affordable compared with most western European cities, and the airport connects you directly to much of Europe.

**The country.** Portugal is safe and welcoming, and English is widely spoken at the university and around the city.
